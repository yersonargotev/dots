# Recover User-Local Provider publication through immutable receipts

User-Local Providers persist captured artifact authority before preparing or publishing executables. Each operation binds the selected home and state identities, declaration, exact resolved artifact, and the complete executable surface. An immutable journal under `dependencies.d/<operation-id>/` stores captured authority, the verified staged postimage, publication progress, a final receipt payload, and a durable completion marker. The completion marker is the terminal commit point. Final payloads without that marker remain pending and must be verified before recovery can acknowledge them. This avoids treating command presence as proof of receipt completion after a publication or persistence failure.

Publication uses pinned directory descriptors and no-replace operations. Recovery can recognize dots' published inodes and complete the same operation without resolving a different release. A foreign command, link, destination, or changed authority fails closed and preserves the evidence and external state. Even a validated older `herdr-custom` public link must be moved explicitly before an upgrade; replacing that occupied link cannot provide the same external-state preservation guarantee. Providers do not migrate the layout or change shell configuration.

The loader projects completion-acknowledged receipts together with legacy `dependencies.json` records; reading does not rewrite legacy bytes or infer ownership. Immutable per-operation records and home/state locks prevent independent dependency updates from losing unrelated receipts. Previously completed receipts remain audit evidence, so normal satisfaction by existing commands and vendor auto-updates retain ADR 0016's behavior. Read-only plans and dry runs may preview pending recovery but never perform it.

**Consequences**: Interrupted operations retain their verified staging witnesses and receipt history. A partial preparation without a durable ready record is ambiguous and requires explicit preservation or relocation before retrying; it is never adopted or automatically deleted. Retaining this bounded private staging is preferable to a rollback or cleanup that could remove external edits. Filesystems without safe no-replace directory promotion report an execution error with retained recovery evidence.

**Authority and concurrency**: The maintainer-approved contract reserves private staging, journal directories and lock entries to dots during an operation. Those private resources use 0700/0600 modes and phase-by-phase identity checks; the protocol does not claim protection against same-UID processes actively manipulating them inside the critical section. Captured public-parent directories and selected-root ancestry must remain stable during publication. dots validates their pinned identities before and after each publication and retains pending evidence on a detected change. Public leafs remain concurrently editable and are protected by atomic no-replace operations.

The home-wide/state lock boundary covers both pending recovery and PATH-based satisfaction decisions. Held lock descriptors must match their named entries. New directory/lock ancestry is synchronized durably, and mutating retries re-synchronize visible immutable phases before relying on them. Ordinary public/state directory creation preserves 0755 subject to umask and existing permissions; exact private permissions are established on newly created resources. Download and extraction honor cancellation before publication.

Canonical captured authority uses a content-addressed `authority-<sha256>.json` filename. The `captured.json` projection must match that authority when present and may be repaired only from its verified canonical body. This detects structurally valid in-place authority corruption before downloads or publication; it is not authentication against an actor coherently replacing the entire private journal. Applicability and receipt history use physical home/state identity so a symlink alias cannot bypass pending recovery.

Device/inode numbers alone are insufficient: filesystems may recycle an unlinked inode even when a replacement has identical bytes. Every captured object, including directories and symlinks, therefore carries a filesystem incarnation obtained from a pinned object descriptor. Linux combines an export file handle with the boot UUID and `STATX_MNT_ID_UNIQUE` (kernel 6.8 or later). The unique mount ID is never recycled during that boot. Darwin requires the volume's `PATH_FROM_ID` capability, which guarantees persistent non-recycled object IDs, and captures `ATTR_CMN_FILEID` together with the volume UUID. Missing support fails closed before relying on mutation authority. Linux reboot/remount changes the incarnation: pending operations retain their evidence and require explicit resolution rather than automatic adoption. External commands without applicable pending operations and legacy metadata reads do not require these capabilities.

A hash-sealed capture ordinal, allocated under the state lock, orders audit projection across incarnation epochs. Only completed chain heads participate, newest capture wins an existing dependency/provider/path collision, and pending operations cannot displace completed metadata. Zero, duplicate, exhausted or nonincreasing predecessor ordinals fail closed. This ordering never authorizes publication or recovery.
